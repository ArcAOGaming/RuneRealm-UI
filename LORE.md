# Rune Realm — world bible

This document is the working canon for the world behind the game. It is an
internal writing source, not page copy. The site may quote a line or reveal a
fragment, but it should never flatten this into an explainer or tell the whole
truth at once.

## The first truth

The Realm was alive before anybody divided it into kingdoms.

Fire moved through root and blood. Water remembered every shape it had held.
Air carried names farther than roads could carry bodies. Stone kept the weight
of the dead and did not surrender it. Where those currents crossed, creatures
appeared: not animals with magic laid over them, but pieces of the world that
had learned hunger, fear, loyalty and play.

The old peoples lived beside them. They fought them when they had to. They
buried them when they could. What they did not do was number every creature,
measure every river, or insist that a life was only real once it had been
entered into a ledger.

The Corporation changed that.

## The Corporation

No surviving record agrees on the Corporation's first name. Its oldest seals
have been ground away and replaced so often that most people simply call it
what it calls itself: the Corporation. A name would suggest there could be
another. It prefers to sound inevitable.

It arrived as roads, medicine, measured harvests and machines that could keep a
settlement lit through the winter. It offered safety from wild creatures and a
single standard of life between distant regions. Then the roads acquired
gates. The medicine acquired terms. The lights would only burn for registered
homes, and a registered home could be inspected, relocated or emptied.

The Corporation does not believe itself cruel. Cruelty is inefficient. It
believes that an unmeasured life is a defect in the system, that choice is the
unpredictable interval before a correct decision, and that the Realm would be
peaceful if every living thing occupied the place assigned to it.

Its machines are called **Units** by its own officers and **Hollows** by nearly
everyone else. Some are built. Some were once creatures. Their element has been
drawn out and replaced with a clean artificial current that never disobeys.
They do not rage. They correct.

Its campaign is not conquest in the old sense. The Corporation calls it the
**Alignment**: the slow conversion of a living world into one legible system.

## The six currents

Four currents are obvious because they make the visible world.

- **Fire** is appetite, change and the courage to spend what cannot be
  recovered.
- **Water** is memory, adaptation and the patience to become a different shape.
- **Air** is distance, language and motion without ownership.
- **Stone** is endurance, consequence and every promise made physical.

They are not personalities and they are not moral camps. A fire creature can
be patient. A water keeper can be merciless. The current describes how a thing
meets the world, not what it chooses to do in it.

Two other currents touch the Realm without belonging to its ordinary matter.
They are rare enough that most people know them only as bad omens, saint tales
or Corporation redactions.

**Light is revelation.** It exposes, multiplies, accelerates and burns away
distinction. It can uncover a lie or erase the shelter that made honesty
possible.

**Dark is keeping.** It conceals, preserves, quiets and gives shape to absence.
It can protect a fugitive or keep a wound from ever reaching the air.

Neither is good. Neither is evil. Light without mercy becomes surveillance;
Dark without release becomes a tomb. Their place in the present conflict is
not yet understood, and they should remain glimpsed long before they are
explained.

## Death, the scroll and the returning rune

A wild creature cannot be reasoned out of defending its ground. To form a bond
with one, a keeper must defeat it completely. The heart stops. The elemental
current leaves the body. This is death, not sleep and not a gentler word chosen
for it.

The rite that follows is called **the Returning**.

A scroll is laid beside the body and marked with the creature's last true
signs: the shape of its current, the place it fell, the wound that ended it and
the name it answered to, if it had one. The scroll does not contain a soul. It
holds a path—a memory precise enough for the scattered creature to find its way
back.

Runes are spent to open that path. A rune is a promise given geometry; when it
is broken in the rite, the promise becomes force. The keeper gives that force
direction and calls the creature home.

What returns is neither a possession nor exactly the creature that died. It
remembers the defeat. It carries a thread of the keeper's current beside its
own. The bond makes growth, revival and battle possible, but it also makes each
responsible for what the other becomes. A companion can be commanded. It
cannot be made loyal by the rite alone.

The Corporation performs a version without scrolls. It strips the current,
copies the useful reflexes and brings the body back obedient. This is how a
living creature becomes a Hollow. The resistance considers destroying a Hollow
an act of mercy; whether a second Returning can restore one remains an open and
dangerous question.

## The four factions

The factions are older than the current resistance, but the Alignment forced
them into the same war. Joining one is an oath of method and kinship. It is not
a claim that one element should rule the others.

### Sky Nomads — Air

The Nomads keep no capital the Corporation can seize. Their routes run across
high bridges, weather stations, abandoned lift rails and paths that exist for
one season. They carry news between isolated settlements and take particular
pleasure in turning Corporation signal towers into instruments of confusion.

Their companion line begins with **Airbud**. Nomads say an Airbud chooses the
next road before its keeper has admitted they are ready to leave.

### Aqua Guardians — Water

The Guardians protect springs, flooded archives and the old channels beneath
the cities. They remember the names that official records have removed. Their
sanctuaries are built to change: a door today may be a reservoir tomorrow, and
an invading force that memorizes the map has already lost.

Their companion line begins with **WaterDoge**. It is playful around clear
water and uncharacteristically still when a memory has been disturbed.

### Inferno Blades — Fire

The Blades began as furnace workers and wardens of controlled burns. They know
the difference between destruction and release. Where the Corporation builds
an extractor, a Blade learns which single support will make the machine consume
itself. Their victories are visible from the horizon, which is both a warning
and a liability.

Their companion line begins with **FireFox**. Its flame is hottest when it is
protecting something, a fact the Corporation's combat models continue to
misclassify as aggression.

### Stone Titans — Stone

The Titans keep the buried places: vault roads, quarry temples, seed chambers
and the foundations of towns removed from modern maps. They do not retreat
quickly, but every retreat is engineered to leave shelter behind. The oldest
resistance halls still stand because a Titan decided where the first stone
would carry the last one's weight.

Their companion line begins with **Rockpup**. Young Rockpups collect fragments
of ruined structures and become furious when those fragments are taken away.

## The open Realm

The world should feel larger than the conflict, even where the conflict has
marked it. The player travels through regions where ordinary life continues:
berry gardens beneath ruined turbines, trade on half-flooded roads, children
cutting harmless runes into slate, and wild creatures nesting inside machines
that were meant to erase them.

Corporation territory is clean, vertical and overlit. Distance is measured,
movement is announced, and every useful surface carries an instruction. Wild
territory is not automatically safe; it is simply allowed to be itself.

The long arc is a fight over who gets to define order. Battles against the
Corporation recover routes, creatures, records and people. Arena battles among
keepers are training, ritual and rivalry—not the war itself. A keeper may win
every sanctioned fight and still have done nothing to free the Realm.

## Story language

The world is arcane, but its people are not vague for effect. They know the
names of tools, the cost of a rite and what a machine does to a village. Mystery
comes from partial knowledge and old damage, not from replacing concrete nouns
with fog.

Use ritual language for moments that alter a life: swearing, Returning,
revival, the breaking of a rune. Use plain language for oppression. The
Corporation evicts, extracts, registers and kills; it does not merely “cast a
shadow.”

The public story should reveal itself as evidence: recovered field notes,
damaged orders, an oath remembered differently by two factions, a companion's
reaction to a place. Do not put this document into a character's mouth.

## Canon still deliberately open

- Who first discovered the Returning, and what did it cost them?
- Can a creature refuse to return? What happens to a keeper who forces the
  path open anyway?
- Is the Corporation controlled by people, an inherited system, or both?
- What are Runes made from, and why can the Corporation manufacture an
  imitation current but not a true one?
- What did Light and Dark do during the earliest Alignment?
- Can a Hollow be restored, and would the restored creature forgive the person
  who brought its body back?
- What physical event divided the present regions of the open world?

Those questions are story space, not missing answers to fill casually. New
features should resolve them only when the resolution creates more play than it
removes.
